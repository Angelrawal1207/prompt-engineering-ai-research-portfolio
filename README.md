## 🧠 Prompt Engineering Techniques Demonstrated

This portfolio demonstrates several important prompt-engineering techniques:

### Role Prompting
Assigning the AI a specific professional role such as:

- Marketing Manager
- Financial Analyst
- HR Manager
- Operations Manager
- Business Analyst
- Research Assistant

### Context Setting
Providing relevant information about the business, target audience, industry, budget, or research topic.

### Constraints
Adding limitations such as:

- Budget
- Target audience
- Number of recommendations
- Output length
- Required categories

### Structured Output
Using tables and defined fields such as:

`Problem | Evidence Required | Action | KPI`

### Zero-Shot Prompting
Asking the model to perform a task without providing examples.

### Few-Shot Prompting
Providing examples before asking the AI to classify or generate a new response.

### Prompt Chaining
Breaking a complex task into interconnected steps where the output of one prompt becomes the input for the next.

### Iterative Prompt Refinement
Improving a vague prompt by progressively adding context, constraints, audience, platform, and output requirements.

### Source Verification
Requiring claims, statistics, publications, and other research information to be checked against reliable sources.

### Hallucination Control
Explicitly instructing AI not to invent:

- Statistics
- Sources
- Research papers
- Authors
- Market information
- Missing business data

### AI Self-Critique
Asking AI to review its own output for weaknesses, assumptions, missing information, and unsupported claims.

## 🔍 Key Learning

The portfolio demonstrates that the quality of an AI response depends significantly on the quality and structure of the prompt.

A broad prompt such as:

> "Tell me about AI in PhonePe."

can produce a generic response.

A more effective prompt specifies:

- **Who** the AI should act as
- **What** exactly should be analyzed
- **Which areas** should be covered
- **What evidence** is required
- **How the output** should be structured
- **Which information** must be treated as assumptions
- **What needs to be verified**

This approach makes AI output more useful, structured, and suitable for research and business analysis.

---

## 🛡️ Research & Verification Principle

AI-generated information should not automatically be treated as verified information.

The research framework used in this portfolio emphasizes:

**AI Output → Source Identification → Verification → Evidence Evaluation → Final Use**

Particular attention is given to fast-changing business metrics such as market share, valuation, transaction volumes, and financial information. These figures should be accompanied by an appropriate date and source before being used as current information.
